There's a particular kind of quiet around grain elevators — the scale of
them dwarfs whatever's parked nearby, but there's surprisingly little
noise to match the size.[^1]

![Example photo](/assets/images/DSC00986.JPG)

Most of the concrete ones still standing along old rail lines were built
well before anyone driving past them today was born.[^2] The style
hasn't needed to change much since.

If you want to see how a post like this one is put together — the image
up top, the sizing and column classes available in the body — there's a
walkthrough elsewhere on the site.[^3]

[^1]: A working elevator is loud when it's actively loading or unloading grain — augers, blowers, rail cars — but sits nearly silent the rest of the time, which is most of the time. Just a clarifying aside, not a citation.
[^2]: Reinforced concrete silo construction like this became the North American standard in the early 20th century. More on the general history: [Silo — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silo).
